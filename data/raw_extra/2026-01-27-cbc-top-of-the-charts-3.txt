New York and its Localities are #1 in Taxing, #3 in Spending

The reality is straightforward: States and localities compete for residents and businesses. Tax and spending policies are a core part of that competition. Comparing states and localities by their tax and spend policies is important to understanding each jurisdiction’s competitiveness.

New York State and its localities are among the nation’s top taxers and highest spenders. They collect the highest taxes per person and the second highest per $1,000 of personal income. They also are the third highest spenders per capita in the country. Combined with affordability challenges, public safety concerns, and uneven public services, these fiscal facts threaten New York’s competitive advantage for residents and businesses.

In 2023, the most recent data available, New York State and its localities:1

Collected the highest taxes per capita and second highest per $1,000 of personal income in the nation; 
New York collected $12,495 per capita, 11 percent higher than second place North Dakota ($9,784);
New York collected $153 per $1,000 of personal income, 52 percent higher than the U.S. average; and
Per-capita collections were 34 percent higher than Connecticut, 37 percent higher than New Jersey, and 40 percent higher than California;
Collected the second highest personal income tax per $1,000 of income among all states; 

112 percent higher than the national average; and
35 percent higher than Massachusetts, and 54 percent higher than California; 
Collected the highest corporate taxes per $1,000 of state gross domestic product (GDP) in the nation;

40 percent higher than New Jersey and 50 percent higher than Connecticut; and
Spent more per capita—$15,894—than 47 states;

40 percent higher than the national average—$11,385;
17 percent more than Massachusetts, 32 percent more than New Jersey, and 92 percent more than Florida; and
Spending like New Jersey would save $76 billion, Massachusetts $45 billion, and Connecticut $129 billion.

New York will not become more affordable and competitive by increasing taxes. To best compete for residents and businesses, New York should enhance its value proposition. 

Taxes are one part of the value proposition equation. New York will not and does not need to be a low- or even average-taxing and spending state. But, given their relative standing, New York State and its localities should hold the line and preferably reduce taxes. 

To improve their competitive value proposition also requires them to enhance the quality of vital services, prioritize services that boost affordability and quality of life, and increase government efficiency.
