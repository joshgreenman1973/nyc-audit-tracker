DOWNLOAD BRIEF: New York, Still Top of the Charts

As New York State and New York City kick off their fiscal year 2026 budget processes, there surely will be calls to increase both spending and taxes. But, New Yorkers and their elected public officials must consider two crucial points.

First, New York State and its localities already have the highest taxes and the second-highest spending per capita in the nation. Second, New York’s relative attractiveness to residents and businesses is threatened by flexibility in a changing economy, peoples’ pandemic experiences, affordability challenges, and legitimate concerns about public service quality and safety.

In 2022, the most recent data available, New York and its localities:1

Had the nation’s highest taxes both in collections per capita and per $1,000 of income; 
Collected the most personal income tax per $1,000 of income among all states; 
114 percent higher than the national average; and
20 percent higher than California, the second highest state; 
Climbed to the top spot for corporate tax collected per $1,000 of a state’s gross domestic product (GDP), from the sixth highest in 2021;
Three-fold increase from 2021 was driven by the creation of the New York State Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET), which provides a work-around to the State and Local Tax (SALT) deduction cap included in the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) of 2017 and resulted in the shifting of some tax burden from personal income to corporate income;2
Spent more per capita—$15,368—than every other state except for Alaska;
Almost 50% higher than the national average ($10,622 per capita); and
More than competitors—4 percent more than California, 39 percent more than New Jersey, and 97 percent more than Florida.

To continue to compete for residents and businesses, New York needs to enhance, not diminish, its value proposition. New York will not become more affordable by increasing taxes and spending. New York State and its localities should hold the line and ultimately reduce taxes, prioritize and improve the quality of vital services that truly deliver, and increase government efficiency. New York will not and does not need to aspire to be a low- or even average-taxing and spending state. But a competitive value proposition requires delivering high-quality services and bringing our taxes and spending more in line with neighbors and competitors.
