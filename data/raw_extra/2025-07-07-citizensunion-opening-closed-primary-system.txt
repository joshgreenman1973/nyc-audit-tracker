Fact Sheet: Opening New York City’s Closed Primary System

• Over 1 million “independents” are excluded from voting in the NYC primary o Under the status quo, only voters registered with a political party can participate in the most crucial election. o These unaffiliated voters are the fastest-growing segment of the electorate.

• Unaffiliated voters are young and diverse o Nearly half of unaffiliated voters in NYC are under 40. o The majority of unaffiliated voters in NYC are minority voters; more than a third are Black and Hispanic. o Young voters of color are the most likely to be registered without a party affiliation.

• They want to participate – but are shut out o Unaffiliated voters are politically engaged and want to vote in primaries. o When excluded from primaries, they’re less likely to vote in general elections.

• Closed primaries empower a small group of voters and Super PACs o In low turnout primaries, mayors are effectively chosen by fewer than 10% of electorate. o The money and power of Super PACs is at its peak in low turnout partisan primaries.

• Opening the primary strengthens democracy and representative government o Allowing more voters to participate leads to elected officials who better reflect the city. o Campaigns must reach a broader base of voters, reducing polarization and encouraging coalition-building.

• This reform brings us in line with the rest of the country o New York City is an outlier – nearly all U.S. cities use a version of open primaries. o Open or nonpartisan primaries have helped elect mayors like Karen Bass in LA, Michelle Wu in Boston, and Brandon Johnson in Chicago. o Candidates will still appear with their party affiliation and be able to seek support from party organizations.

• This reform complies with strict voting rights protections under state law o A report by former U.S Attorney General Loretta Lynch found that “there is no basis for finding that [open primaries] would in any way diminish the ability of any protected class to participate in the political process.”

• Now is the time o A wave of civic energy is building, especially among young voters. o We shouldn’t waste this moment — NYC is ready to open its elections to all voters.
